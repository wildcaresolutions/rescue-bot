BAT RESCUE AND CARE GUIDELINES
Bats are rabies vector species and must NEVER be hand led with bare
hands. Always seek professional help before attempting to rescue a bat.
Follow these guidelines for proper bat rescue and care.

ALWAYS INCLUDE: IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTES
* NEVER handle a bat with bare hands. Always wear gloves and eye
protection when rescuing a bat.
* Seek medical attention immediately if bitten or scratched.
* Keep pets and children away from the bat at all times.
* Bats are rabies vector species, and they are the primary carriers of
rabies in the Bay Area and Marin County. This means any bat coming
into contact with a persons bare skin must be euthanized and
tested for rabies. DO NOT put yourself at risk! Always wear gloves
when encountering a bat.

Determining Bat's Age
1. Neonate (Pink and Naked)
* If the bat is tiny, pink, and naked, and his eyes are sealed shut,
he is a neonate and requires immediate warmth and care.
2. Juvenile (Small, Wobbly, Soft Fur, Eyes Open)
* If the bat is small, wobbly, with short, soft fur, and eyes open, he
is a juvenile and needs immediate warmth and care.
3. Adult or Subadult (Fully Furred, Eyes Open, Vocal)
* If the bat has full fur, bright open eyes, and is vocal, he is an
adult or subadult bat.
* NEVER handle an adult bat with bare hands.

BAT RESCUE AND CARE SECTION 2: Rescuing an Adult Bat
Always Contact Professional Help First
* Before attempting to rescue an adult bat, contact trained
professionals like your local Animal Control organization to
ensure both your safety and the bats well-being.
When to Rescue an Adult Bat
* Any adult bat found on the ground and unable to fly is in trouble
and needs immediate medical care.
* If the bat does not fly away when you approach, the bat likely needs
to be rescued.
* NEVER handle an adult bat with bare hands.
Rescue Procedure (ONLY after contacting professional help):
1. Prepare a Box or Bin:
* Have abox or binwith pre-punched air-holes readywith alid
to prevent escape.
* Line the containerwith a towel.
* Make sure the box or bin is big enough for the bat to move
around.
* Have the box beside you as you attempt the rescue.
2. Protect Yourself:
* Wear heavy glovesto protect your hands.
* Use a small towel or other cloth to provide additional protection
for you and the bat.
* Have a tool like a trowel (small shovel) or wooden spoon
available to pick up the bat.
3. Rescue the Bat:
* Throw the small towel over the bat, noting where his head is.

* Do NOT pick up the bat. You willscoopthe bat into the box.
* Use awooden spoonorsmall shovelor similar implementto
scoopthe bat into the box.
* Do NOT wrap the bat tightly. The bat should be ableto move
away from the towel once in the box.
4. Keep the Bat Safe:
* After placing the bat in the box, put thelidon securely.
* Do NOT offer food or waterto the bat, as he willnot eat or
drink when stressed, and wet fur will make the bat cold..
5. Bring the Bat to Professionals:
* Transport the bat to Wild Careor your nearest wildlifecare
center as soon as possible.
* If you must keep the bat overnight, place the container in a
warm, dark, quiet room that is closed off from the rest of the
house. Do NOT open the box. Do not peek at the bat.

BAT RESCUE AND CARE SECTION 3A: Rescuing a Baby Bat
* Any baby bat on the groundrequires immediate rescueand
warmth.
* Cover your handswith gloves or a towel to avoid directcontact with
the bat.
* NEVER handle a bat of any age with bare hands.
Rescue Procedure for Baby Bats:
1. Prepare a Box or Paper Bag:
* Pre-punchair holesin the box before placing the bat inside.
* Line the box withlight material(e.g., towel).
2. Rescue the Bat:
* Gently toss or place a small towel over the baby bat.
* Use awooden spoonorsmall trowelor similar implementto
gently scoop the baby bat into the box.

* Do not wrap the bat tightly. Place the bat with the towel in the
box and close it securely.
3. Do NOT offer food or water.
4. Search for other babies. If you find one, there maybe others
nearby.
5. Bring the baby bat to Wild Careor your nearest wildlifecare center as
soon as possible.
6. If theres a delay in getting the bat to expert help, place the bat in a
warm, dark, quiet place(e.g., closet) with a supplemental heat
source.
* Keep the heat source onone sideof the box, allowingthe bat
to move away if it gets too hot. Please ask for more information
on keeping him warm.

BAT RESCUE AND CARE SECTION 4: A Bat Trapped Inside the House
* If a bat is trapped insidea home or structure withchildren, pets, or
people who may not be able to confirm contact with the bat, the bat
must beeuthanized and tested for rabies.
* Do not let the bat out. Close off the room containingthe bat and call
local animal controlimmediately.
* If you cant reach animal control, contact your local health
department.
* Wait for professional assistance to capture the bat.
Steps to Handle a Trapped Bat:
1. Seal the Room: If the bat is trapped in an unoccupiedroom, close
the door.
2. Contact Wild Carefor advice at 415-456-7283. If afterhours, keep
the room sealed until you can reach them.
