BOBCAT RESCUE AND CARE
Overview:
Abobcat(Lynx rufus) is a medium-sized wild cat nativeto North America. Here's a
detailed description:
* Size: About twice the size of a domestic cat. Adultstypically weigh between15
to 30 pounds, with males larger than females.
* Body: Sturdy and muscular with long legs andlargepaws.
* Tail: Short and bobbed, only about5 to 7 incheslong-this is how the bobcat
gets its name.
* Fur: Soft, thick fur that istan to reddish-brown, often withdark spots or
streaksfor camouflage. The underside is usually lighter, and the insides of the
legs and belly are white with black spots.
* Face: Distinctive tufted ears (shorter than those of a lynx), whiskered cheeks,
and awhite ruffaround the face.

Baby Bobcats
* Size: At birth, bobcat kittens are tiny-about thesize of a domestic kitten,
weighing8 to 12 ounces.
* Fur: Covered insoft, fluffy fur, typically grayish or buff-colored with faint
spots or stripesthat become more pronounced withage.
* Eyes: Kittens areborn with their eyes closed, whichopen at around7-10
days. Their eyes are typically blue when they first open and change to yellow or
greenas they grow.
* Ears: Small, rounded, and later develop the characteristic black tips and white
spotsseen in adults.
* Tail: Short and stubby with ablack tip on top andwhite underneath, just like
adult bobcats.

To Determine the Age of the Bobcat.
Observe the Bobcat's size and physical features to determine her age.
1. Baby (Neonate):No fur or very short, thin fur. Eyessealed shut. Helpless and
unable to walk.
2. Juvenile:Small, kitten-like, withsoft, fluffy fur, typically grayish or buff-colored with
faint spots or stripes that become more pronounced with age.
3. Adult or Subadult:Large, sturdy and muscular withlong legs andlarge paws.
Short and bobbed tail only about5 to 7 incheslong. Distinctive tufted ears
whiskered cheeks, and awhite ruffaround the face.

To Determine if an Adult Bobcat is Ill or Injured:
To determine if the adult Bobcat is ill or injured, observe the following signs:
* Difficulty breathing or labored breathing
* Visible wounds or bleeding
* Limping or inability to use a limb
* Discharge from the eyes or nose
* Lethargy or unresponsiveness
* Unusual vocalizations or behavior
RESCUE STEP 1: Determine if the animal requires rescue
* A baby (neonate) Bobcat that is alone and away from her mother ALWAYS
needs rescue.
* A juvenile Bobcat that is alone and away from her mother ALWAYS needs rescue
* An adult Bobcat that doesnt run away from you ALWAYS needs rescue.
RESCUE STEP 2: Determine if wildlife professionals should be called in

* Baby Bobcat: No. With care and proper protective gear, you should be able to
safely rescue this baby Bobcat yourself
* Juvenile Bobcat: No. With care and proper protective gear, you should be able to
rescue this young Bobcat yourself
* Adult Bobcat: Yes. Adult Bobcats can be hazardous to handle. IMPORTANT:
Always call for professional assistance before attempting to rescue.
RESCUE STEP 3: Have anot her person assist. Adult Bobcatsare very strong and
even young Bobcats have sharp teeth and claws that can cause injury. Rescue will be
more successful and safer with a second person assisting.
RESCUE STEP 4: Collect protective gear as needed
Always wear thick gloves, eye protection, and long sleeves.
Have two thick towels ready.
RESCUE STEP 5: Prepare a container for the rescued animal
ALWAYS INCLUDE: Use a large box or bin with a lid and pre-punched air holes or a
large pet carrier. Line the container with a towel.
Note: If the Bobcat is a baby, you will need to provide supplemental heat if you cant get
him into care quickly.
RESCUE STEP 6: With the container beside you, throw something over the animal
to restrain him.
ALWAYS INCLUDE: Toss a towel over the Bobcat to restrain him, making note of where
his head is.
RESCUE STEP 7: With hands protected, lift the Bobcat from behind. Make sure
the face is pointed away from your body. Place the Bobcat in the box and close
the lid.
RESCUE STEP 8: Does the animal need to be kept warm? Follow instructions if
so.
**IF animal age is adult: No. An adult Bobcat does not need supplemental head
**IF animal age is baby: Yes. A baby Bobcat should have a supplemental heat source.
IMPORTANT: Only place the heat source under half of the container so the animal has
the option to roll off the heat if he gets too hot.
