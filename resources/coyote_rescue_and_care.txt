COYOTE RESCUE AND CARE
Overview:
Acoyoteis a medium-sized wild canine native to North America. Coyotes have aslim,
agile body, typically weighing between 20 to 50 pounds, withlong legs, abushy tail,
andlarge, pointed ears. Their fur is usuallygrayish-brownwithrust-coloredaccents
behind the ears and around the legs, and alighter underside.
Ababy coyote, also called apup, is a small, fuzzyanimal born blind, deaf, and
completely dependent on its mother. At birth, pups weigh about8 to 10 ouncesand
havesoft, dark gray or brown fur, which lightensas they grow. Their eyes open
around10 to 14 days old, revealingblue eyesthatlater turnamber or golden.

Coyotes with Severe Mange
Coyotes with mange often appear thin, scruffy, and sickly, with large patches of bare
skin that may be red, blackened, or covered in sores. In severe cases, they may look
almost hairless, resembling a different species altogether. The irritation can lead to
constant scratching and biting, which worsens the condition and can result in open
wounds and secondary infections.

Mange is a skin condition caused by parasitic mites. While it can look severe, it
sometimes resolves on its own in healthy animals as their immune system responds.
However, treating wild coyotes for mange is rarely feasible for several reasons:

- Reinfection: Coyotes live in family groups. The entire pack and den would need
  simultaneous treatment, or reinfection is inevitable. Trapping an entire pack is
  highly unlikely, and captivity causes extreme stress.
- Habituation: Providing treatment through food habituates coyotes to humans,
  increasing conflict risk.
- Dosing safety: Mange treatments must be carefully dosed by weight. Incorrect dosing
  can cause seizures or death. Other animals could be harmed if they ingest the
  medication.

A common underlying cause of severe mange is secondary poisoning from rodenticides
(rat poison). When coyotes eat poisoned rodents, their immune systems weaken, making
it difficult to fight off mites and allowing mange to become severe or deadly.

What to do if you see a coyote with mange:
1. Keep your distance. Do not approach, feed, or attempt to assist the animal.
2. Observe whether the animal is mobile or immobile.
3. Keep pets away -- mange is contagious to domestic animals.
4. Report the sighting to your local wildlife rehabilitation center or animal control.
