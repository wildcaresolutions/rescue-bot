DEER AND FAWN RESCUE AND CARE
Overview:
Deer are graceful, hoofed mammals known for their slender legs, large ears, and short
tail. Most deer species have a reddish-brown or grayish-brown coat, and males typically
grow antlers, which they shed and regrow each year. Deer are herbivores and are often
found in forests, grassl and s, and meadows.
A fawn is a baby deer.
Determine the age of the deer. Instructions differ depending on the age of the
animal.
Observe the deer's size and physical features to determine her age.
1. Fawn
* Fawns are small withwhite spotson their backs.
* Young adult deer, without spots, are considered adults.

2. Adult or Subadult
* Appearance:Large, long legs, no spots on the back
* IMPORTANT: Adult deer can be dangerous. Do not approach an adult
deer.
RESCUE STEP 1: Determine if the animal requires rescue
* Afawnthat is injured or truly orphaned ALWAYS needsrescue, but it can be
difficult to determine if the fawn is actually orphaned. The mother deer will leave
her baby alone for up to 12 hours while she forages. The fawn stays still and quiet
awaiting her return.
If the fawn is sitting quietly, he is likely not orphaned and does not need help.
A fawn needs assistance if he is showing any of the "5 Cs":
* He's Crying
* He's Coming toward you (walking around or approaching people)
* He's Cold (shivering)
* He's been Caught by a cat/dog/other predator

* He's Covered in blood or insects.
* Mother deer sometimes choose places for their fawns that seem odd or unsafe. It is
always better to leave the fawn alone and wait for her mother to return, but if the
fawn is in an area that seems very unsafe (like a busy parking lot), or is in an
inconvenient location (like under your car), it may be possible to gently lift her and
move her to anot her location within a few feet of where you found her. We
recommend calling for assistance before attempting to touch or move the fawn. Call
us at 415-456-7283.

* It is NOT possible to rehabilitate an ADULT deer.
* In California and many other states, it isillegalto rehabilitate adult deer
* Attempting to capture an adult deer can cause Capture Myopathy, a
condition in which stress caused by capture can result in death or
permanent nervous system damage for the animal.
* If the adult deer is injured, but the injury is not life-threatening, the best
option is toleave adult deer alone. Attempting tocapture an adult deer is
dangerous and can lead to fatal stress for the animal and injury to the
human.
* Do Not Attempt to Rescue an Adult Deer
* If an adult deer is severely injured or immobile, the only viable
option ishumane euthanasia, which should be performedby
professionals.
* Do NOT attempt to rescue or handle adult deer. Alwayscontact
a wildlife professional or animal control for assistance.

RESCUE STEP 2: Determine if wildlife professionals should be called in
* Fawn (baby deer) No, you should not need the help of wildlife professionals or
animal control. If the fawn is injured or if he truly needs rescue, you should be
able to safely rescue this fawn yourself
* Adult deer: Yes, this should be hand led by wildlife professionals or animal
control. IMPORTANT: Never attempt to rescue or interact with an adult deer.

**IF animal age is fawn: ALWAYS INCLUDE: If you confirm that the fawn needs to be

RESCUE STEP 3: Collect protective gear as needed
Fawns may carry fleas and ticks. Wear long sleeves and wear gloves to protect
yourself.
RESCUE STEP 4: Prepare a container for the rescued animal
* Use alarge cardboard boxthat can be closed or anappropriately-sizedpet carrier.
* Punch air holesin the box before placing the fawninside.
* Line the box with atowel or blanket.

RESCUE STEP 5: With the container beside you, throw something over the animal
to restrain her.
* Place a towel or blanket overthe fawn to keep hercalm, noting where her head is.
* Pick her up gentlyaround the middle, tucking herlong legs underneath as you lower
her into the box.
* Do NOT wrap the fawn tightly; allow her to move outfrom under the towel.
*

Do NOT Offer Food or Water

* Keep children and pets away from the fawn. Be veryquiet. Do NOT peek at her, as
human noise and presence is stressful for the fawn.

RESCUE STEP 6: Transport the animal to care as soon as possible.
ALWAYS INCLUDE: Do NOT offer food or water. A stressed fawn will not eat, and food
and water will spill and stick to the fawns fur. Stay quiet and do not peek because
human presence is stressful for the fawn.
