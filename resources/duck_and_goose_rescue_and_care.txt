MALLARDS, DUCK AND GOOSE RESCUE AND CARE
General Information
* If anadult duck or goosedoesn't fly or swim away from you when
approached, she needs rescue.
* A Mallard is a type of duck
IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTICE:
These birds can potentially carry Avian Influenza (HPAI). Always wear eye
protection, and gloves, and a maskwhen handling them. After handling,
thoroughlywash hands and shoeswith soap and waterto prevent
transmission of the virus.
SECTION 1: HOW TO RESCUE AN ADULT DUCK OR GOOSE
* Safety Note:These birds can potentially carry Avian
Influenza (HPAI). Always wear eye protection, gloves, and a
maskwhen handling them. Wash hands and shoes thoroughly
afterward.
1. Preparation:
1. Large box that can be closed, with air holes, or apet carrier
and a towel or paper towels inside.
2. Wear gloves, long sleeves, eye protection, and amask(to
prevent HPAI transmission).
3. Have a helperif possible. Adult ducks and geese canbe
difficult to handle alone.
2. Steps:
1. Cover the bird:
* Use atowel or blanketto cover the bird, noting where
his head is. You want to pick him up from behind.
2. Lift the bird:
* Hold the birdfirmly and gentlywith both hands, likea
football,

1. Hold the bird low next to your hip to prevent him
from reaching your face with his bill.
2. Uncover the birds head to allow him to breathe
3. Use your other hand to gently control the neck and
head.
 DO NOT wrap the bird tightly.
*
* Place the duck/goose in the box. Leave the towel or
blanket inside with the bird, butmake sure its loose
enough that the bird can move out from under it.
* Close the box tightly.
3. Keep the bird in a warm, quiet placeuntil you canget it to
Wild Care or the nearest wildlife care center.

HOW TO RESCUE A DUCKLING OR GOSLING (BABY DUCK OR
GOOSE)
1. Call for trained assistance. Ducklings and goslingsare very fast
and agile and they dont want to be captured. If they are in the water,
rescue may be very difficult.
1. Prepare a small boxlined with a light towel or papertowels. Ensure
the box hasair holesand a secure lid (ducklings/goslingscan jump).
2. Observe: Watch the bird for10 minutesto confirmhes truly alone.
The mother may be nearby. Only rescue a duckling or gosling if he is

truly alone.
3. Capture:
1. Use along-hand led netor alight towel/t-shirtfor
capture.
2. Have your box ready.
3. Maneuver the duckling/gosling into an area where
you can use the net to scoop him out of the water or
toss the towel/t-shirt over him.
4. Use both hands to gently scoop up the bird and the
material/net, lifting it immediately to prevent
drowning.
5. If using a towel, ensure the bird'shead is exposed
for breathing.
6. Handle the birdgently but firmly.
7. Place him in the boxand remove the wet
material/net to prevent the bird from getting
cold.
8. Close the box or bag securely and bring it to
Wild Care immediately.
Transport:
1. Keep the baby warm:
* Warmth is essential for ducklings and goslings. Please
ask for further instructions on keeping the duckling or
gosling warm.

* Only put the heat source on one side of the
container to give the bird the option to move off the
heat if he gets too warm.
2. Bring the bird to Wild Care or your closest wildlife care center
as soon as possible.
* DO NOT offer food or water.
* Keep children and pets awayto minimize stress onthe
bird.
SECTION 2: HOW TO RESCUE A BABY DUCKLING OR GOSLING
THAT HAS FALLEN INTO A SEWER GRATE
1. A duckling or gosling that is trapped under a grate is in danger and
needs immediate assistance.
2. Call your local animal control organization for assistance. In Marin
County, call Marin Humane at 415-883-4621 and press 1 to reach
an officer. Outside of Marin County, visit Animal Help Now.org or
https://www.google.com/search?q=local+animal+control
a. The local police non-emergency line may also be an option.
3. Keep an eye on the duckling until help arrives.
4. You may be able to use a long-hand led net or something like a tennis
racket to gently scoop the duckling out of the sewer.
a. Be very care ful and gentle when attempting to capture the bird.
b. Bring the bird to your nearest wildlife hospital immediately after
rescue as he may be chilled and hungry.
