HUMMINGBIRD RESCUE AND CARE
SECTION 1: Any Hummingbird on the Ground Needs To Be Rescued
* Important: Any hummingbird found on the groundmustbe rescued, regardless of age.
These birds should never be on the ground, and it is considered an emergency.
* Nighttime: If it's nighttime, keeping the bird warmovernight is better than leaving her
outside. However, seeking emergency help immediatelyis the priority.

SECTION 2: How to Rescue a Grounded Hummingbird
1. Prepare a Small Box with a Lid or a Paper Bag:
* Line it withtissue, paper towel, or alight cloth.
* Pre-punch air holesin the box before placing thebird inside.
2. Handling the Hummingbird:
* Use alight clothorpaper towelto cover your handswhen lifting the bird.
* Gently pick up the bird, along with whatever shes gripping. Hummingbird feet
are fragile, and you should avoid pulling them away from what they are holding.
* Close the bag or box to prevent escape
3. Warmth:
* Provide a heat source ononly one sideof the boxor container. Ask for further
instructions on keeping her warm.
4. Important:
* DO NOT feedthe bird anything. Feeding a cold, sick, or injured hummingbird can
cause the bird to aspirate (choke) and increase the risk ofhypothermia.
* Transport to care immediately.
5. If There Is a Delay in Getting the Bird to Care::

* Place the bird in the box or paper bag andkeep her in a warm, dark, and quiet
place.
* Do not peek at the bird and keep children and petsaway.

SECTION 3: Emergency Feeding for Adult Hummingbirds
* If you have taken in an adult hummingbird and cannot find qualified local help you
may need to feed the bird to keep her alive. If it is between the hours of 9AM-9PM PST,
please call Wild Care at415-456-7283before attempting.
* Formula: Mix4 parts waterto1 part plain white sugar(best emergency
formula).
* If the bird is alert, you can place a hummingbird feeder inside the container.
* If the bird is too weak or young to self-feed, use a small syringe, pipette, or
dropperto offer sugar water. You can also use aspoonor asaturated cotton
swab.
* Critical Reminders:
* Never use honey, artificial sweeteners, or other substitutes-thesecan be
harmful.
* Never force-feeda hummingbird. Only feed ifabsolutelynecessary.
* Always seek expert interventionas soon as possible. Every hummingbird case
is urgent.

SECTION 4: How to Rescue a Hummingbird Trapped Indoors
* If a hummingbird is able to fly but is trapped inside, try the following:
1. Place a brightly-colored flower pot or hummingbird feeder just outside the
nearest external door where the hummingbird can easily see it. Leave the door
open as the clear route for escape.
2. Turn off lights and close blindsto help guide thebird toward the open door.
3. Exit the roomand leave the door open until the birdflies out.

SECTION 5: Has the Nest of Baby Hummingbirds Been Abandoned?
* Important:99.9% of the time, the nest is not ab and oned. The mother hummingbird is
still caring for her babies, and she visits for less than 10 seconds to feed them. This
avoids attracting predators, but makes the mother hard to spot.
* Once babies have feathers and can regulate their body temperature, the mother
spends less time at the nest.
* Do not attempt to remove a nestor baby hummingbirds. Its extremely unlikely that
they are orphaned.
* NEVER attempt to feed baby hummingbirds yourself.
* Call Wild Care at 415-456-7283for guidance beforeintervening.
