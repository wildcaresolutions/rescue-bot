RODENT RESCUE AND CARE
A rodent is a small to medium-sized mammal characterized by a pair of continuously growing
incisor teeth in both the upper and lower jaws. These teeth are sharp and used for gnawing on
a variety of materials like wood, seeds, and plants.
Key Physical Characteristics:
* Teeth: Prominent front incisors that never stop growing
* Body: Typically compact and covered in fur
* Ears: Usually rounded and proportional to the head
* Tail: Can be long and thin (like a rat's) or short and furry (like a vole's) or fluffy like a
rodents
* Feet: Clawed toes for digging or climbing
Examples of Rodents:
* Mouse / Mice
* Rat
* rodent
* Beaver
* Gopher
* Vole
* Chipmunk
To Determine the Age of the Rodent
Observe the rodent's size and physical features to determine her age.
1. No Fur (Pink/Naked):
* Neonate rodents (eyes sealed shut, small, pink) require immediate warmth and care.
2. Short, Soft Fur, Small & Wobbly, Eyes May Be Open or Closed:
* Juvenile rodents (small, soft, thin fur, wobbly) also require immediate warmth and care.
3. Fully Furred with Bright Eyes:
* Adult or Subadult rodent.
* ALWAYS INCLUDE: Adult rodents can bite. Always wear gloves when attempting to
handle an adult rodent.
To Determine if the Animal Requires Rescue
* A baby (neonate) rodent ALWAYS needs rescue.
* A juvenile rodent on the alone or away from mother and siblings ALWAYS needs
rescue
* An adult rodent that doesnt run away from you ALWAYS needs rescue.

To Rescue a Baby Rodent (Rat, Mouse, Vole, Gopher etc.)
1. Prepare a small container with a lid and pre-punched air holes. A box or small plastic
container will work.
2. Line the container with something soft, like a washcloth or a paper towel.
3. Wear gloves. Even baby rodents may bite, or they may have fleas or ticks on them.
Always protect your hands when rescuing animals.
4. Use a small towel or other material to pick up the baby rodent.
5. Provide heat for the baby rodent. Place a heating pad on low to one side of the container
to allow the animal to move off the heat if he gets too hot.
6. Bring the baby rodent to care as soon as possible.
To Rescue an Adult Rodent (Rat, Mouse, Vole, Gopher, etc.)
1. Prepare a small container with a lid. An adult rodent can chew out of a cardboard box,
so you will need a plastic or metal container. Remember rodents can jump well, so make
sure the lid fits tightly.
2. Line the container with something soft, like a washcloth or a paper towel.
3. Wear gloves. Adult rodents have large teeth and strong jaws. Their teeth can cause
significant injury. Wear gloves, and have a towel or cloth ready as extra protection when
handling the animal.
4. Throw a small towel over the animal, paying attention to where his head is.
5. Use a towel or other material to pick up the rodent, keeping the head and teeth pointed
away from your body.
6. Bring the animal to care as soon as possible.

RESCUE STEP 10:Transport the animal to care as soonas possible.

ALWAYS INCLUDE: Do NOT offer food or water. A stressedanimal will not eat, and
food and water will spill and stick to the birds feathers. Stay quiet and do not peek at
the animal because human presence is stressful for wildlife.
