SONGBIRD RESCUE AND CARE GUIDE
This guide provides essential information for rescuing and caring for songbirds.
A songbird typically has the following physical characteristics:
* Size:Small to medium, usually between 2 to 8 inches in length.
* Body Shape:Slender and lightweight, often with a rounded head and streamlined
form for agile flight.
* Beak:Generally short and pointed, though shape can vary-thin and sharp for
insect-eaters, or short and thick for seed-crackers.
* Wings:Moderate in length, often rounded or pointed, suited for flitting and short bursts
of flight.
* Tail:Varies in shape-some have long, fanned tails, others short and square; often
used for balance and steering.
* Feet:Adapted for perching, with three toes forward and one back (an isodactyl),
allowing a strong grip on branches.
* Plumage:Often brightly colored or distinctly patterned, especially in males; females
may have duller, camouflaged feathers for nesting.
* Eyes:Relatively large and round, positioned on the sides of the head for wide field of
vision.

SECTION 1: Determine the age of the songbird
* No feathers (pink/naked): Hatchling. Requires immediatecare.
* Downy feathers (white and fluffy with pink skin visible) or pin-feathers (you
can see the feathers in their sheaths): Nestling. Requires immediate care.
* Adult feathers with some downy feathers and a short tail: Fledgling. May not
require rescue.
* All adult feathers: Adult songbird. Adult songbirds have shiny feathers and long
tails. They should be able to fly easily. Requires rescue if injured or unable to fly.
SECTION 2: Determine if the bird requires rescue
1. When to rescue an Adult Songbird:


Rescue an adult songbird if he exhibits anyof the following:
1. Falling over or unable to fly
2. Does not fly away when approached.
3. Bleeding
4. Caught by a cat, dog, or other animal
5. Wing drooping or tweaked upward
6. Weak, shivering, or lethargic.
7. Feathers fluffed up.

Any hatchling or nestling songbird on the ground requires immediate
rescue and care
A fledgling songbird on the ground may not need rescue. Only rescue a fledgling
songbird if he is ill, injured or orphaned.

How to Determine if a Fledgling Songbird Needs to Be Rescued
Recognizing a Fledgling
* A fledgling bird has fully developed feathers, but the tail is noticeably shorter than that of
adult birds.
* A fledgling bird may still have some fluff on the top of his head
* A fledgling bird can perch, hop, or walk, but may seem less coordinated than adult birds.
* A fledgling bird is learning to fly, but cant fly yet. He may take short hopping or fluttering
flights to practice.

* A fledgling bird is not in need of rescue unless injured, ill, or orphaned.
* Healthy fledglings that require no immediate intervention are:
* Bright-eyed and healthy-looking
* Hopping or fluttering
* Parent birds are present and feeding the baby
If the fledgling exhibits any of the following, he needs rescue:
o Falling over or unable to hop or walk.

o Caught by a cat, dog, or other animal.

o Weak, shivering, or lethargic.

o Wing drooping or tweaked upward.

o Feathers fluffed up.

o Bleeding.

o Does not hop or flutter away when approached.

If the fledgling is not showing any of the above symptoms, assess the situation to
determine if the bird needs rescue:
How to Assess the Situation
* Check for Dangers:Is the fledgling in a safe location?Are there cats nearby, or is he in
a park with dogs and children running about?
* Observe the Parent Birds: Watch from a distance of50 feet or more for 60-90 minutes.
If pare nts are feeding the fledgling, no intervention is needed. However, if the pare nts
are NOT feeding the fledgling after 60-90 minutes of observation, the bird needs to be
rescued.
* Observe the Fledgling: If the bird is active (hopping, fluttering), leave him be and
observe for 60-90 minutes. When possible, you can check the birds droppings for signs
of malnutrition or orphaning.
* Clear white-ish poop or green bile indicates that the baby bird is not being fed
and may be orphaned
* Color in the poop (brown, purple, etc.) suggests that the parent birds are feeding
the baby, and he should be returned to his original location.
*

It is NOT true that parent birds will reject theirbaby if a human has touched him.
Birds will not reject their baby because of human handling.
* If you do not see the parent birds attending to their fledgling after lengthy
observation, the fledgling should be rescued.

SECTION 3: How to Rescue a Songbird
How to Rescue an Adult Songbird

1. Prepare for rescue:
* Cover hands with light gloves or a tea towel to prevent bites or scratches.
Songbirds may also carry parasites like feather lice.
* Use a box or paper bag with pre-punched air holes and a lid that can be closed
completely. Line it with paper towels, tissue, or light material.
2. Capture the bird:
* Gently toss a lightweight net or towel over the bird.
* Use both hands to gently scoop the bird up with the net/towel.
* Hold the bird gently but firmly.
3. Place the bird in the box:
* Place the bird, along with the net or towel, in the box or bag. Do not keep the bird
wrapped tightly in the material.
* Close the box tightly and bring the bird to Wild Care or your closest wildlife care
center as soon as possible..
* If there is any delay in getting the bird to care, keep the bird in a warm, dark,
quiet location away from children and pets. Do not peek at the bird.
4. Important reminders:
* Do NOT offer food or water- the bird will not eator drink in captivity, and
water or food can spill and soak the birds feathers.
* Bring the bird to Wild Care or your closest wildlife care center as soon as
possible..
How to Rescue a Baby Bird
1. Prepare for rescue:
* Wear light gloves or use a tea towel to handle the bird.
* Use a box with a secure lid or paper bag. Pre-punch air holes.
2. Capture the baby bird:
* Gently place a lightweight net or towel over the bird and scoop him up carefully.
* Hold the bird gently but firmly.
* Place the bird, along with the net or towel, in the box or bag. Do not keep the bird
wrapped tightly in the material.
* Close the box securely.
3. Keep the baby warm:
* If the bird is a hatchling or nestling, warmth is essential. Please ask for further
instructions on keeping them warm.
Only put the heat source on one side of the container to give the bird the option to move off the
heat if he gets too warm.
4. Transport the bird:
* Bring the baby bird to Wild Care or your closest wildlife care center immediately.

SECTION 4: What to Do If an Entire Nest of Baby Birds Has Fallen
1. Rescue the nest:
* Babies in the nest will likely have been injured in the fall. They will need urgent
care at Wild Care or your nearest wildlife hospital.
* Wear light gloves and collect the entire nest with the babies inside.
* Place the nest in an appropriately-sized box lined with cloth or paper towels.
2. Immediate care:
* Bring the nest to Wild Care or the nearest wildlife hospital.
* Do not offer food or water.
* Keep the baby birds warm until they can be brought to care. Please let me know
if you need further instructions on this. Do not overheat the baby birds.
3. ALWAYS INCLUDE: Never attempt to renest a single babybird in a different nest.
Parent birds will not tend more than one nest.
