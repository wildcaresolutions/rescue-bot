**WILD TURKEY RESCUE AND CARE**

## **General Information**

If an adult wild turkey does not run or fly away when approached, it may need rescue.

### **IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTICE**

Wild turkeys can inflict serious injuries with their feet, wings, and beaks. Adult male turkeys (toms) have sharp spurs on the backs of their legs that can cause deep scratches or puncture wounds. Always use caution when handling a turkey's legs and feet.

Wear gloves, long sleeves, eye protection, and a mask when handling wild turkeys. After handling, thoroughly wash your hands and shoes with soap and water.

## **SECTION 1: HOW TO RESCUE AN ADULT WILD TURKEY**

### **Safety Note**

Wild turkeys can potentially carry Avian Influenza (HPAI). Always wear eye protection, gloves, and a mask when handling them. Wash hands and shoes thoroughly afterward.

Adult male turkeys may have sharp spurs on their legs. Be especially careful when handling the bird's feet and legs to avoid injury.

### **Preparation**

* Large box that can be closed, with air holes, or a pet carrier.  
* Towels or paper towels for the bottom of the container.  
* Gloves, long sleeves, eye protection, and a mask.  
* A helper if possible. Adult turkeys can be large, strong, and difficult to handle alone.

### **Steps**

**Cover the bird:**

* Use a large towel or blanket to completely cover the bird.  
* Approach from behind whenever possible.  
* Note the location of the head before attempting to pick up the bird.

**Lift the bird:**

* Hold the wings securely against the body to prevent flapping.  
* Support the bird's body with both arms, holding it firmly but gently against your side.  
* Keep the bird low and away from your face.  
* Use caution around the feet and legs. Adult male turkeys may have sharp spurs that can cause injury.  
* Do not hold the bird by the legs, neck, wings, or tail.

**Place the turkey in the carrier:**

* Place the bird gently into the box or carrier.  
* Leave the towel or blanket inside, but ensure it is loose enough that the bird can move freely.  
* Close the container securely.

### **While Awaiting Transport**

* Keep the bird in a warm, quiet, dark place until transport is possible.  
* Keep children and pets away.  
* Do not repeatedly check on the bird, as human interaction causes stress.  
* Do not offer food or water. Stress can prevent eating and drinking and may increase the risk of aspiration.

### **Transport**

Bring the turkey to WildCare or the nearest licensed wildlife rehabilitation center as soon as possible.

# **HOW TO RESCUE A TURKEY POULT (BABY WILD TURKEY)**

If a healthy, uninjured baby wild turkey (poult) is found alone but calling loudly, look for the hen and brood before rescuing, since turkey families often become temporarily separated and can frequently be reunited on-site. That situation is more common with poults than true orphaning.

## **General Information**

Turkey poults can run surprisingly fast and often hide in vegetation when frightened. Before attempting a rescue, determine whether the poult is truly orphaned, injured, or in immediate danger.

### **Observe First**

Watch the poult from a distance for at least 30 minutes whenever possible. Mother turkeys often leave their young temporarily while foraging and may return when people leave the area.

Only rescue a poult if:

* It is injured.  
* It has been confirmed orphaned.  
* It is trapped or in immediate danger.  
* It is weak, unable to stand, or unresponsive.

## **SECTION 1: HOW TO RESCUE A TURKEY POULT**

### **Preparation**

* A small box or pet carrier lined with a light towel or paper towels.  
* Air holes and a secure lid.  
* Gloves.  
* A lightweight towel or small net if needed for capture.

### **Capture**

1. Have the box prepared and nearby before attempting capture.  
2. Approach slowly and calmly.  
3. If necessary, use:  
   * A lightweight towel, t-shirt, or small net to gently cover or contain the poult.  
   * Your hands gently; scoop up the bird.  
4. Support the poult's body with both hands.  
5. Handle the bird gently but firmly.  
6. Place the poult in the box immediately.  
7. Remove any wet towels or capture materials from the container to prevent chilling.  
8. Close the box securely and transport the bird to WildCare as soon as possible.

## **Transport** **Keep the Poult Warm**

Warmth is essential for young turkey poults. Place the heat source on only one side of the container so the poult can move away if it becomes too warm.

### **During Transport**

* Keep the container in a quiet, dark location.  
* Keep children and pets away.  
* Do not repeatedly check on the bird, as this increases stress.

### **DO NOT**

* Offer food or water.  
* Attempt to raise the poult yourself.  
* House the poult with domestic poultry.

### **Bring the bird to WildCare or your nearest wildlife rehabilitation center as soon as possible.**

## **SECTION 2: HOW TO RESCUE A TURKEY POULT TRAPPED IN A STORM DRAIN, SEWER, OR OTHER ENCLOSED SPACE**

A trapped turkey poult is in danger and may need immediate assistance.

### **Call for Help**

Contact your local animal control agency or wildlife rescue organization for assistance.

In Marin County, call Marin Humane at 415-883-4621 and press 1 to reach an officer.

Outside Marin County, contact your local animal control agency or wildlife rehabilitation center.

The local police non-emergency line may also be able to assist in some situations.

### **While Waiting for Help**

* Keep the poult in sight if it can be done safely.  
* Monitor for the return of the mother turkey and other poults.

### **If Rescue Is Possible**

You may be able to gently retrieve the poult using:

* A long-handled net.  
* A towel.  
* Another soft, non-abrasive object that can safely lift the bird.

Be extremely gentle during capture.

After rescue:

* Place the poult in a secure box.  
* Keep it warm and quiet.  
* Transport it to a wildlife rehabilitation center immediately for evaluation.
